**2. Impact of Built Environment on Patient Falls**
*   **Citation:** Pati, D., Valipoor, S., Lorusso, L., et al. (2021). The Impact of the Built Environment on Patient Falls in Hospital Rooms: An Integrative Review. *Journal of Patient Safety, 17*(4), 273-281.
*   **Type:** Peer-reviewed Academic Journal
*   **Abstract:** This integrative review identifies and evaluates scientific evidence on how the built environment in hospital rooms affects patient falls. A systematic search of CINAHL, PsychINFO, PubMED, and Web of Science (1990-2017) yielded 30 articles. Key environmental elements examined include room/bathroom configuration, bed height, flooring, lighting, and handrails. The review found that the evidence level for many environmental factors is not high enough for robust confidence in design decision-making, recommending more quantitative and causal studies.
*   **Subjects:** Built Environment, Accidental Falls, Patients' Rooms, Human.

*   **Abstract:** This article describes a 10-year program that successfully reduced inpatient falls by 46%, resulting in approximately 568 fewer falls annually. **5. Characteristics of Patient Falls in Hospital**
*   **Citation:** Ostensvik, E. (2007). Characteristics of patient falls in hospital. *Nordic Journal of Nursing Research & Clinical Studies / Vård i Norden, 27*(3), 13-18.
*   **Type:** Peer-reviewed Academic Journal
*   **Abstract:** A retrospective study of 295 fall incident reports in a Norwegian hospital (fall rate: 1.8 per 1000 bed days). Falls were significantly higher at night. Patients aged 70+ accounted for 77% of falls. Key characteristics at the time of falling included disorientation (24%), impaired mobility (40%), and toileting needs (37%). Walking (45%) and falling from bed (40%) were the most common activities. Serious injuries occurred in 8% of falls.
*   **Subjects:** Accidental Falls Evaluation, Inpatients, Age Factors, Hospitals Norway.

**11. Multilevel Factors Influencing Falls of Older Patients in Hospital**

**13. Risk Factors for In-hospital Patient Falls: A Retrospective Analysis**

*   **Abstract:** A retrospective descriptive study of 677 patient fall incidents in a Queensland hospital (2015-17). Most falls (98%) occurred in inpatient units, caused minimal harm, and were largely related to toileting activities. Older age groups (75-94) had the highest proportion of falls.
